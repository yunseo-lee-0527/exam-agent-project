## Q4. Short Answer

"Therbligs" are a set of 18 elementary motions developed by Frank and Lillian Gilbreth, used to analyze and classify human movements in work tasks. They represent fundamental actions like "search," "find," "grasp," "hold," and "release," and were introduced as part of micromotion analysis to standardize and improve the efficiency of work methods beyond simple time studies.

## Q6. Short Answer

Let's use the Subtraction framework. This framework works by identifying an essential component or feature of a product or service and intentionally removing it, then finding new ways to achieve the core function or value proposition without that component. This forces re-evaluation and often leads to simpler, more elegant, or even entirely new solutions.

For example, consider a traditional desktop computer. Applying Subtraction, we could remove the physical hard drive. The innovation would be a "diskless" or "cloud-native" computer where all storage and often processing power are handled remotely in the cloud. This leads to innovations like thin clients, Chromebooks, or fully streamed gaming platforms, offering benefits like reduced hardware costs, easier maintenance, and greater accessibility, albeit with reliance on internet connectivity.

## Q7. Concept Comparison

Necessary variability arises from inherent factors like changing demand, diverse task complexity, or natural human performance fluctuations. It is essential for system adaptability and often cannot be eliminated, such as a customer service representative adjusting their tone based on a caller's mood. Unnecessary variability, however, stems from poor design, lack of training, or inconsistent methods, leading to wasted effort and defects. An example is an assembly line worker performing a task differently each time due to unclear instructions. The key difference is that necessary variability contributes to robust system function, while unnecessary variability detracts from efficiency and quality, and should be minimized through standardization and process improvement.

## Q8. Concept Comparison

The KJ Method (Affinity Diagram) focuses on structuring unstructured qualitative data, typically gathered from brainstorming or interviews. Its primary goal is to organize ideas into natural groupings to reveal underlying themes, patterns, and root causes of a problem, moving from divergence (idea generation) to convergence (affinity grouping).

In contrast, the Concept Fan technique is used to broaden and then refine the scope of a problem or solution. It starts with a core problem statement, then radiates outwards to explore related areas before converging back to specific solutions. Its goal is to ensure a comprehensive understanding and explore a wide range of potential solutions by systematically varying the abstraction level.

Both techniques help structure problems, but the KJ Method organizes existing data to reveal insights, while the Concept Fan strategically expands and contracts the problem definition to identify solution avenues.

## Q10. Application

Three distinct Therbligs involved are:
1.  **Grasp (G):** Picking up the circuit board or a small screw.
2.  **Position (P):** Orienting the circuit board correctly for screw attachment or aligning the screw with its hole.
3.  **Assemble (A):** Attaching the screw to the circuit board using the power tool.

To improve efficiency, we can focus on 'Position (P)' for the screws. Instead of loose screws that need to be oriented by hand, using a *screw feeder* or *pre-loaded screw magazine* would present the screws to the power tool in the correct orientation. This significantly reduces or eliminates the manual positioning Therblig for each screw, minimizing fumbling, standardizing the motion, and speeding up the assembly process, thereby enhancing overall efficiency.

## Q11. Essay

Scientific Management aimed to systematically redesign work by reducing variability, standardizing methods, and separating planning from execution. Taylor focused on optimizing tasks through time studies, while the Gilbreths extended this with motion studies and Therbligs to eliminate unnecessary movements, dramatically improving efficiency in areas like bricklaying. Its lasting impact lies in establishing foundational industrial engineering practices for efficiency and standardization. However, its major limitations include a mechanistic view of workers, often neglecting human factors, motivation, and the necessity of certain variability in complex tasks. This rigid approach can hinder organizational flexibility and worker autonomy.

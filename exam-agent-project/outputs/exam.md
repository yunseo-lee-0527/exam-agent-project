# Scientific Management Midterm Exam

Duration: 75 minutes  |  Total: 100 points

## Instructions

- Answer all questions in the space provided.
- Use concepts and terminology from the lecture materials.
- For application and essay questions, justify your reasoning explicitly.
- Point values are shown for each question.

## Q1. Short Answer (5 points)

What was a primary goal of scientific management regarding work system design?

## Q2. Short Answer (5 points)

Briefly explain the primary purpose of brainstorming in the engineering problem-solving process.

## Q3. Short Answer (5 points)

In the context of work systems, distinguish between "necessary" and "unnecessary" variability.

## Q4. Short Answer (5 points)

What are "Therbligs" and who introduced them in the context of work study?

## Q5. Short Answer (5 points)

What is the primary purpose of the KJ Method (Affinity Diagram) in problem structuring?

## Q6. Short Answer (5 points)

Select one of the systematic innovation frameworks (Addition, Subtraction, Alternate, Combination, Transposition) and explain how it works. Then, provide a novel example of an innovation that could arise from applying this framework to an everyday product or service.

## Q7. Concept Comparison (10 points)

Compare and contrast 'necessary variability' and 'unnecessary variability' within the context of work systems, providing a brief example for each.

## Q8. Concept Comparison (10 points)

Compare and contrast the KJ Method and the Concept Fan technique, highlighting their primary goals and how they approach the task of understanding and structuring engineering problems.

## Q9. Application (15 points)

Imagine you are tasked with innovating the traditional public bus stop experience. Apply the 'Subtraction' framework from the systematic innovation methods to redesign a key aspect of this service. Describe the resulting innovative concept and explain how the Subtraction framework was utilized to achieve it. Focus on removing an essential component.

## Q10. Application (15 points)

Consider a manufacturing worker assembling a small electronic device, which involves picking up a circuit board, orienting it, attaching three small screws with a power tool, and then placing the finished device into a box. Identify at least three distinct Therbligs involved in this specific sequence of actions. For one of the identified Therbligs, propose a concrete method improvement that would reduce or eliminate it, explaining how this change would enhance efficiency.

## Q11. Essay (20 points)

Analyze the core aims and methodologies of Scientific Management, encompassing both Taylor's foundational principles and the Gilbreths' contributions to motion study, in the context of work system redesign. Evaluate its profound impact on industrial efficiency and critically discuss its inherent limitations, specifically considering how its approach may overlook human factors and necessary variability in modern work environments.
